## Spectacle Is Not Histrionics

To settle this and avoid being nitpicked by wannabe psychologists and DSM-5
cope, I want to preface the following: ***spectaclemaxxing*** is not ordinary
histrionicism, as many might initially believe. For example, in the
self-internalized state that a *jestermaxxer* would inhabit, it is implied that
there is a deliberate act of conscious engagement in humorful, socially
rewarding, and validation-seeking behavior through either charisma or accepted
self-deprecation.

*Jestermaxxing* and ***spectaclemaxxing***, then, can be described as
meta-cognitive, in that they involve a modeled awareness of performing and
adjusting in real time.

Histrionicism, by contrast, operates less as a consciously constructed paradigm
and more as an affective compulsion, driven by reassurance-seeking and
emotionally reactive pattern systems rather than deliberate strategic
engagement. There is inherent unintelligence, impulsivity, and shallowness
baked into histrionics that I would take offense at having ascribed to me.

However, the spectacle I yearn to be and cultivate is all intentional.
Deliberately modeled, knowing full well the stochasticity on which I am sizing
my decisions.

There’s a PnL (Profit and Loss) factor to this; I could always have a model on
the losing side of a decision trade. A rational masochism that comes with my
exhibitionism, knowing full well the trade I take is ultimately to maximize my
utility.

## The Exhibitionist Instinct

I love attention—a lot! I love performance! I love being performative! I love
wielding an unnecessary (in most cases) oversized, elitist lexicon in my
everyday dialogue—something my ML research friends jokingly call
“tokenmaxxing.” None of it is accidental.

For every disclosure of my oddness, for every contrarian stance I hold—from my
flesh to my rhetoric, to whom I fall in love with—for every time I dump lore
about myself, for every niche, weird moment I share, it is all in the pursuit
of being a spectacle. To be a natural contrarian for the love of the game. To
push the limits of performance.

Every moment of openness is a placed and sized trade.

In many friend groups I gradually grow closer to, I develop an insatiable urge
for attention—and, more importantly, for performance. It is not even the want
of performance driven by others’ expectations. I don’t desire to perform to
please people (I am actually quite stubborn about whose opinions I actually
value). But I love to perform for its own sake. As a form of utility, as a form
of embodiment, as a form of gnosis. If spectacle is proof of existence, then
dialing it down is existentially threatening. And so, to be invisible is akin
to death.

I can go on forever, trapping myself in an Id–Ego samsaric cycle of sycophancy
about my innate nature to be the exhibit for all to witness, from skin to
thought. You might call out the irony in my descriptions. Earlier, I mentioned
that histrionism is characterized by over-emotionality and impulsivity.

But here’s the thing: I am deeply aware of these mechanisms—aware enough, in
fact, to critique them and call them out, bringing a kind of genuine humility
upon myself. And so, even at my most irrational, this merely becomes just a
mispricing-of-a-trade risk.

## Sizing the Spectacle

In the art of treating performance and spectacle-procuring as a volatile but
lucrative asset, one is to tie performance to vulnerability. I practice what I
call “strategically-placed vulnerability,” in which I calibrate and use
intuition to initiate a trade expressed as vulnerability and disclosures,
especially in new social groups.

I have mentioned here “sizing decisions,” “Kelly mathematics,” and many
analogies to financial trading in my approach to ***spectaclemaxxing***. What better
idea is there than to introduce the mathematics I am referencing: the Kelly
Criterion.

The Kelly Criterion provides a formulation for placing the optimal bets of an
arbitrary currency under uncertain conditions. We define arbitrary currency as
any medium of value through which utility is transacted—whether capital,
attention, status, or social positioning. In simpler terms, it is the idea and
application of risking just enough to gain utility without risking
destabilization.

Suppose you risk a fraction of an arbitrary currency when probabilistic
outcomes are repeated over many iterations, with:

- Probability of success:

$$
p
$$

- Probability of failure:

$$
q = 1 - p
$$

- Net payoff multiple on success (i.e., you gain “Net payoff multiple” units
  for every 1 unit risked):

$$
b
$$

Then the optimal fraction of the total arbitrary currency you risk is:

$$
f^* = \frac{bp - q}{b}
$$

The Kelly Criterion seeks not to provide the highest average return per
decision, but rather to identify the fraction that allows the arbitrary
currency to grow fastest over time without self-destruction. This is done by
maximizing expected logarithmic growth—the average rate of compounding over
time when returns are multiplicative rather than additive—thereby explicitly
accounting for compounding, volatility, and survival over repeated exposure
rather than simply chasing the highest average return per decision.

Let:

- The fraction of the arbitrary currency allocated:

$$
f
$$

- The rate of return on the opportunity, expressed as a percentage:

$$
R
$$

- The notation of the expected value (the weighted average of all possible
  outcomes of a random variable):

$$
E[\cdot]
$$

- The logarithmic transformation capturing multiplicative growth:

$$
\log(1 + fR)
$$

The Kelly objective is therefore denoted as:

$$
\max_f E[\log(1 + fR)]
$$

In financial markets, this might look like allocating a calculated portion of
your portfolio to a high-volatility stock—large enough to meaningfully benefit
from your expected edge (your estimated positive expected value before the
outcome is known), but small enough that a wrong move does not compromise
overall stability. You don’t go all in; you size the trade according to the
strength of your edge (a positive expected value under uncertainty) and the
degree of uncertainty involved.

***Spectaclemaxxing***, for me, operates the same way. For every contrarian stance,
for every reveal of vulnerability, it is a calculation between sounding
interesting and becoming a spectacle while preserving stability. Every
disclosure of vulnerability is a sized exposure—large enough to generate
visibility, differentiation, and energetic response, but constrained enough to
preserve coherence and structural stability, at least in theory.

In that regard, vulnerability can be modeled as a form of relational capital.

## Performative “Proof”

Let:

- Relational capital before disclosure at time $t$:

$$
W_t
$$

- Relational capital after disclosure at time $t + 1$:

$$
W_{t+1}
$$

- Probability that disclosure deepens connection:

$$
p
$$

- Probability that disclosure reduces perceived stability or trust:

$$
q = 1 - p
$$

- Relational gain multiple if connection deepens:

$$
b > 0
$$

- Relational loss multiple if connection destabilizes:

$$
a > 0
$$

- Fraction of relational capital exposed:

$$
f \in [0, 1]
$$

If disclosure succeeds:

$$
W_{t+1} = W_t(1 + fb)
$$

If disclosure fails:

$$
W_{t+1} = W_t(1 - fa)
$$

We size disclosure to maximize long-run growth of relational capital:

$$
\max_f \ p\log(1 + fb) + q\log(1 - fa)
$$

We are going to want to solve for the optimal fraction of relational capital we
risk, $f$. So, we take the derivative of the objective function

$$
p\log(1 + fb) + q\log(1 - fa)
$$

with respect to $f$:

$$
\frac{d}{df}
$$

Using the chain rule:

$$
\frac{d}{df}\log(1 + fb) = \frac{b}{1 + fb}
$$

$$
\frac{d}{df}\log(1 - fa) = \frac{-a}{1 - fa}
$$

Therefore the derivative of the objective is:

$$
\frac{pb}{1 + fb} - \frac{qa}{1 - fa}
$$

Set terms equal to zero:

$$
\frac{pb}{1 + fb} - \frac{qa}{1 - fa} = 0
$$

Rearrange:

$$
\frac{pb}{1 + fb} = \frac{qa}{1 - fa}
$$

Cross-multiply:

$$
pb(1 - fa) = qa(1 + fb)
$$

Expand both sides:

$$
pb - pbfa = qa + qafb
$$

Rearrange, again:

$$
pb - qa = pbfa + qafb
$$

Factor $f$:

$$
pb - qa = f(pba + qab)
$$

Factor $ab$:

$$
pb - qa = fab(p + q)
$$

Since $p + q = 1$, this simplifies to:

$$
pb - qa = fab
$$

Solve for $f^*$:

$$
f^* = \frac{pb - qa}{ab}
$$

If relational capital compounds over repeated interactions, then exposure
should scale with perceived edge rather than impulse.
